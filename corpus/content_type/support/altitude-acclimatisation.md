# Altitude Acclimatisation — Basics for Trekkers

This guide covers the basics of altitude acclimatisation for the multi-day treks Aurora Outfitters customers are likely to attempt: Annapurna Circuit (Thorong La 5,416 m), Tour du Mont Blanc (peaks under 2,500 m), and similar routes.

## Why altitude matters

Above ~2,500 m, atmospheric pressure drops and the partial pressure of oxygen in your blood drops with it. Your body adapts (acclimatisation) over a period of days. Pushing too high too fast leads to **Acute Mountain Sickness (AMS)** — headache, nausea, fatigue, sometimes worse. Untreated severe AMS can progress to **High-Altitude Cerebral Edema (HACE)** or **High-Altitude Pulmonary Edema (HAPE)**, both life-threatening.

## The rules

Standard altitude rules above 3,000 m:

- **Don't sleep more than 500 m higher than the previous night.**
- **Take a rest day every 3-4 sleeping days.**
- **Climb high, sleep low.** A day hike to higher altitude that returns to a lower camp helps acclimatisation.
- **Listen to symptoms.** Mild headache and breathlessness are normal. Increasing symptoms after rest are not.

These rules govern itineraries on the Annapurna Circuit (`09-guide-annapurna-circuit.md`) and similar treks. They are not particularly relevant on Tasmania (`06-guide-tasmania-overland.md`) or the Tour du Mont Blanc (`08-guide-tour-du-mont-blanc.md`) which sleep below 2,500 m.

## Symptoms to watch

**Mild AMS (acceptable, monitor):**
- Mild headache
- Nausea (no vomiting)
- Mild fatigue
- Breathlessness during exertion only

**Moderate AMS (don't ascend further):**
- Persistent headache after rest
- Persistent vomiting
- Resting heart rate consistently elevated
- Difficulty sleeping

**Severe AMS (descend immediately, regardless of itinerary):**
- Severe persistent headache unresponsive to ibuprofen
- Ataxia (poor coordination — try the heel-to-toe walking test)
- Confusion, altered mental status
- Resting breathlessness

## Drugs

- **Diamox (acetazolamide):** Standard prophylactic on the Annapurna Circuit and similar high-altitude treks. Start 24-48 hours before going above 3,000 m, continue for the duration. Talk to your doctor; some people have side effects.
- **Ibuprofen:** Useful for mild altitude headaches.
- **Dexamethasone:** Emergency drug for HACE — carry under guidance only.

This document is informational. Always consult your doctor before high-altitude travel.

## Pace and exertion

- **Day-of-arrival rule:** No vigorous exercise on the day you arrive at a new high-altitude camp.
- **Easy pace:** "Slow enough to hold a conversation in full sentences." If you're gasping mid-sentence, you're going too fast.
- **Eat and drink:** Hydration drops at altitude. Drink 4-5 L of water per day. Eat carbohydrate-heavy meals.

## Common mistakes

- **Sleeping at the highest point of the day.** Don't. Always camp lower than your highest point if possible.
- **Skipping rest days because you feel fine.** Acclimatisation is invisible until it isn't. Take the rest days.
- **Ignoring symptoms because of summit pressure.** The mountain will be there next year.
