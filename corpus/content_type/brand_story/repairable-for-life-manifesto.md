# Repairable for Life — Aurora Outfitters' Design Manifesto

Repairable for Life is one of our four brand pillars (alongside Built for the Worst Weather, Trail-Tested by Experts, and Carbon-Negative by 2030). It is a *design* commitment, not a service commitment. Repair-for-Life (`17-repair-for-life-programme.md`) is the service; Repairable for Life is what makes that service possible.

## The four brand pillars

| Pillar | What it commits to |
|---|---|
| Built for the Worst Weather | Every product spec'd against the conditions the worst customer journey will throw at it. |
| Trail-Tested by Experts | Every product field-tested by named ambassadors with hundreds of trail-miles in the category. |
| Repairable for Life | Every product designed so its known failure points are user-serviceable or workshop-repairable. |
| Carbon-Negative by 2030 | The portfolio strategy that the other three pillars enable. |

## What "designed to be repairable" means in practice

Every product Aurora Outfitters designs goes through a **Repairability Review** before it ships. The review tests:

- **Known failure points.** Have we identified them? Are they user-serviceable or workshop-repairable?
- **Component independence.** Can the failed component be swapped without destroying the rest of the product?
- **Replacement supply chain.** Do we have a 7-year-post-retirement supply commitment for that component?
- **Repair training.** Have we written the repair workflow for the workshop team?

Products that don't pass the review get reworked or don't ship. We've killed two product launches between 2021 and 2024 over this.

## Examples

**The Aurora TerraTrek 7 (`01-product-terratrek-7.md`)** — its known failure points are sole wear (year 3-5), eyelet pull-through (year 4+), and leather stitching at the heel (year 5+). All three are repairable at the workshop. The sole is even user-replaceable for a competent cobbler with our service parts.

**The Aurora Skyline 45L (`02-product-skyline-45l.md`)** — its known failure points are hip-belt foam compression (year 4-6), shoulder-strap stitching (year 5+), and frame stay corrosion at the entry/exit eyelets (year 7+ in coastal use). All three have replacement components available.

**The Aurora Cumulus 2P (`04-product-cumulus-2p-tent.md`)** — its known failure points are pole breaks at the joint sleeves (year 3-5), fly-fabric UV degradation (year 6+ at altitude), and mesh tears at peak corners. All three are workshop-repairable.

## What's different about how we design

Most outdoor brands design for **production cost**. We design for **lifecycle cost**.

A 10% increase in production cost that delivers a 50% increase in average product lifespan is a clear win on the lifecycle-cost math. We've been willing to take that trade on every product Repair-for-Life enrols.

The catch: we have to *commit to it for 7+ years post-retirement*. That commitment is the moat. Cheap brands can match our specs; they can't match our spare-component supply chain because they didn't build it.

## Why this matters for our customers

If you participate in Repair-for-Life:

- Your Aurora TerraTrek 7s last 8-12 years instead of 3-4.
- Your Aurora Skyline 45L lasts 10-15 years instead of 4-6.
- Your Aurora Helios lasts ~12 years if you do annual workshop wash + loft restoration.

The math: 22 kg CO2e of new gear in year 1; ~37 kg CO2e total over 10 years. The industry average is 89 kg CO2e over 10 years. We've reduced the customer's gear-lifetime footprint by 58%.

## Why this matters for the brand

Repairable for Life is what lets us claim **Carbon-Negative by 2030** (`18-carbon-negative-2030.md`) honestly. Without the design-for-repair commitment, the maths doesn't close. The pillar is operationally significant, not just marketing.

## What we ask of customers

- **Bring stuff back.** Use Repair-for-Life. Don't throw away what we can repair.
- **Tell us when it breaks.** The data drives the next iteration's design changes.
- **Don't modify in ways that void repairability.** Heavy custom modifications can change the failure mode in ways we can't service.
