# Trail Guide — Annapurna Circuit

The Annapurna Circuit is the original Himalayan trekking circuit. 160-230 km depending on starting point and modern road bypasses, traditionally walked in 12-21 days. Crosses the 5,416 m Thorong La pass — the headline section. Tea-house infrastructure throughout means light packs; altitude is the constraint, not gear weight.

## Quick facts

- **Distance:** 160-230 km (route choice dependent)
- **Days:** 14-18 standard (longer = better acclimatisation)
- **Max elevation:** 5,416 m at Thorong La pass
- **Best season:** Oct - mid-Nov; Mar - Apr
- **Sleeping:** Tea-houses every 2-4 hours of walking
- **Permits:** ACAP + TIMS (issued in Pokhara or Kathmandu)
- **Guide requirement:** Independent trekking permitted again as of 2024; check current Nepal rules before you go.

## Sample 16-day itinerary

The acclimatisation logic dominates. Plan more rest days, not fewer.

**Day 1 — Besisahar to Bhulbhule (10 km).** Drive-in or walk-in.
**Days 2-4 — Bhulbhule to Chame.** Gentle altitude gain.
**Day 5 — Chame to Pisang (2,650 m → 3,200 m).** Last village before serious altitude.
**Day 6 — Pisang to Manang (3,540 m).** Critical acclimatisation village.
**Day 7-8 — Rest days in Manang.** Day hikes to Ice Lake or Kicho Tal.
**Day 9 — Manang to Yak Kharka (4,050 m).**
**Day 10 — Yak Kharka to Thorong Phedi (4,450 m).**
**Day 11 — Thorong Phedi to Muktinath via the pass (5,416 m → 3,800 m).** Long, hard day.
**Days 12-16 — Descent to Jomsom + flight or jeep out.**

## Altitude — the only thing that matters

Acute Mountain Sickness affects 30-50% of trekkers above 4,000 m. The 14-day-minimum itinerary exists to mitigate it. See `22-altitude-acclimatisation.md` for the rules.
